Three ICT audit engagements conducted across CBK-supervised digital credit providers in Kenya. Each engagement covered the full stack of IT general controls — from governance and access management through to application-layer controls, vendor oversight, and operational documentation. All findings were reported against CBK ICT guidelines and mapped to ISO 27001:2022, NIST SP 800-53, and COBIT 2019.

## Most Common Findings

### High severity

- Privileged credentials stored in plaintext — found across all three engagements
- MFA absent or not enforced for privileged and external access
- Dormant user accounts not deprovisioned — active credentials with no assigned owners
- No role-based access control — flat permissions with no least-privilege enforcement
- Sensitive data accessible without audit trail or access logging

### Medium severity

- Segregation of duties gaps — same role authorising and approving transactions
- Unpatched internet-facing endpoints with known CVEs
- File upload functionality unrestricted — no file type or size validation
- No formal change management process for production systems
- Password policy not enforced at system level — complexity rules advisory only

### Low severity

- ICT policy documentation incomplete — key procedures exist on paper but not enforced technically
- Business continuity plan present but not reviewed, tested, or approved by management

---

## Recurring Theme

> A consistent gap between documented ICT policy and actual system configuration was observed across all three engagements. Controls were written into policy but were not enforced at the technical layer.
