## Executive Summary

Investigated a SIEM alert triggered by an inbound email from an
external sender using an unusual top-level domain (`.me`). The goal
was to determine whether the activity represented a genuine threat or
a false positive by analysing the sender domain, email authentication
records, and outbound communication history.

<Callout type="key">
  **KEY INSIGHT:** A suspicious TLD alone is not sufficient evidence of
  malicious intent. Without corroborating indicators, failed authentication
  (SPF, DMARC, DKIM), a payload, or a malicious link, an alert fired on TLD
  alone will generate significant noise. Context is what separates a true
  positive from a false positive.
</Callout>

## Email header analysis

From here i proceeded with email header analysis using public sources. To start my analysis, I separated the sender email into two parts; the user and domain - divided by the `@` symbols, resulting in `eileen` and
'trendymillineryco.me. Domail analysis followed to understand the IP address and the user or organization behind this domain.

## Domain Investigation

**WHOIS - DomainTools**

WHOIS via DomainTools showed that the domain was only available for sale, with no user or organization listed as the registrant and no WHOIS record associated with it.

![domain tools result](/whois-record.png)

**DNS Resolution - URLScan.io, nslookup, ping**

Tools and commands including URLScan.io, nslookup and ping returned no results, meaning I could not resolve an IP address behind the Domain

![urlscan results](/urlscan.png)

**Email authentication - SPF, DMARC, DKIM**

I inspected for SPF, DMARC, and DKIM records, returning no results. No email authentication configuration existed for this domain.

![spf, dmarc, dkim results](/spf_dmarc_dkim-results.png)

**VirusTotal**
After some back and forth with various tools, I decided to check the domain on VirusTotal, which returned no malicious indicators. No vendors had flagged the domain as malicious.
Notably, the domain had been submitted to virustotal 3 days prior to my investigation, and the comments on the details tab all pointed to one thing:

> _"Is trendymillineryco.me a known business partner? No. it looks
> like a random, cheap domain often used by attackers."_

## Email Body analysis and indicators

The body of the email contained some of indicators of phishing:

#### Solicitation of Sensitive Data

For instance, the email requested: "To claim your inheritance send us your banking details immediately". Legitimate business inquiries, especially to a general **support@** Address
never request banking credentials in the first correspondence. this is a clear social engineering tactic.

#### Pyschological Triggere (Urgency)

The attacker used the word **"immediately"** to create a sense of urgency. this attempts to bypass the victim's critical thinking, forcing them to act fast without verifying the source.

## SIEM Pivot: Outbound Communication Check

I returned to the SIEM to check whether the support email had sent anything to the sender, sung the filter below:

```python
sender="support@tryhatme.com" AND recipient="eileen@trendymillineryco.me"
```

There were no results. I ran additional filters targeting any outbound
traffic from the support email, which confirmed that no payment
information was sent to the domain under investigation.

### Classification & Recommendation

Given that there were no attachments, links, or access to the email
to check the return path or test for spoofing, I decided to classify
this alert as a **False Positive**.

**Rule Tuning Recommendation:**

The current detection rule relies solely on TLD, which generates
significant noise without additional corroborating context. The rule
should also check for failed authentication — specifically SPF, DMARC,
DKIM, or S/MIME — as relying solely on TLD without additional
authentication context will generate significant noise. Suggested
conditions before firing:

- `AND` (SPF fail `OR` DKIM fail `OR` DMARC fail)
- `AND` (email contains attachment `OR` email contains URL)
- `AND` sender domain age < 30 days
